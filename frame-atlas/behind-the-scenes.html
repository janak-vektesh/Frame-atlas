Four things behind it

The call sheet

Every shooting day starts with a call sheet: who is needed, where, when, and which scenes will be filmed.

Shot out of order

Films are usually shot by location and schedule, not story order, so a script supervisor tracks continuity down to props and hair.

The slate

The clapperboard marks scene and take, and gives editors a visual and audio sync point for picture and sound.

Foley and the mix

After the shoot, Foley artists recreate footsteps, cloth and props in a studio, then sound teams blend them with dialogue and music.

Set photographs

A day on set

A shoot day runs on a tight clock. The assistant director keeps the schedule while departments prepare the next set as the crew shoots the current one.

Lighting changes take longer than performances. One new camera angle can mean re-lighting the room, which is why coverage is planned carefully.
