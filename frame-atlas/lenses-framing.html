Four ways to frame

Shot sizes

From extreme wide to extreme close-up, shot size controls how close we feel to a character.

Focal length

Wide lenses stretch space and exaggerate depth; telephoto lenses compress distance and flatten it.

Depth of field

Shallow focus isolates a subject; deep focus keeps near and far sharp, as in Citizen Kane (1941).

Thirds and the 180° line

Off-centre subjects feel natural, and staying on one side of an imaginary line keeps screen direction clear.

Through the viewfinder

Pick a lens

Through the glass

Lenses change emotion without changing the actor. A wide lens close to a face feels distorted and anxious, while a long lens from far away feels observed and cool.

Composition guides the eye before anyone notices. Leading lines, foreground frames and empty space all tell the audience where to look and how to feel about it.
