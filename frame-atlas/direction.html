Four ideas from directors

The dolly zoom

Hitchcock’s Vertigo (1958) pulled the camera back while zooming in, bending space so a character’s dizziness becomes the audience’s.

Hiding the monster

When the mechanical shark kept failing, Spielberg showed it as little as possible in Jaws. The suspense worked better than the effect would have.

Many cameras, one take

Kurosawa filmed the battles in Seven Samurai with several cameras at once, capturing chaos that could not be repeated.

Obsessive retakes

Kubrick demanded repeated takes. The bat scene in The Shining is widely reported at 127, a recognised record.

The rehearsal room

Seeing like a director

Directors work in layers. First comes the script, then the visual plan: storyboards, shot lists and rehearsals that decide where each camera will stand.

On set, blocking decides how actors move through space. A step toward the camera or a pause in a doorway can say more than a page of dialogue.
