A camera move is a decision about who is in control: the character, the audience or the filmmaker.

The lens opens onto a moving worldCamera movements

Four ways to move

Pan and tilt

The camera pivots on a fixed point, turning left-right or up-down, like a head turning to look.

Dolly and truck

The whole camera travels on wheels or track. Near things move faster than far things, so the move reveals real depth.

Crane and jib

A boom lifts the camera through space for rising reveals, from a face to the whole city around it.

Handheld

Operators carry the camera by hand to add urgency and instability, common in war films and documentaries.

A move, explained

Choose a move by asking what the audience should feel. A slow push in builds intimacy or dread, a pull back reveals isolation, and a rising crane gives a sense of scale.

Planning matters as much as the move itself. Tracks must be levelled and laid, focus pullers rehearse distances, and actors hit marks so the shot lands once, smoothly.
