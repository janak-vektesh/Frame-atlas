Four tools of the trade

Three-point lighting

A key light shapes the face, a fill softens shadows and a backlight separates the subject from the background.

The Steadicam

Garrett Brown’s Steadicam let operators glide smoothly on foot. It debuted in Bound for Glory (1976) and soon defined Rocky and The Shining.

The Kuleshov effect

Early Soviet experiments showed the same face reads differently depending on the shot beside it, proving editing creates meaning.

LED volumes

Virtual production surrounds actors with real-time LED screens. The Mandalorian (2019) helped popularise the approach.

The craft, unpacked

Methods are shared tools, not rules. Directors and cinematographers borrow, bend and break them to serve a particular feeling.

Technology keeps changing the options, from film stock to digital sensors to LED stages, but the questions stay the same: where do we look, and what do we feel?
